California Agricultural Workforce Development Program

Regional partnerships connecting agricultural employers, education providers, and communities to strengthen workforce skills and career opportunities.

On this pageOverviewWho it's forHow it worksWhat it offersPartnersUpcoming

Overview

Overview

The California Agricultural Workforce Development Program brings together specialty crop employers, community colleges, and other partners to address the skills needed in California agriculture. Its work connects training and curriculum development with industry needs, while supporting career pathways for farmworkers and students.

Regional coordination helps connect education, outreach, and work-based learning with local agricultural employers. The program also supports small farms through connections to relevant technology, tools, and resources.

Who it's for

Who it's for

The program serves farmworkers and students developing agricultural careers, employers identifying workforce needs, educators building relevant training, and small farms seeking support. Opportunities are coordinated through participating regional organizations and education providers.

How it works

How it works

Regional coordinators bring employers, colleges, and community partners together to identify workforce needs and organize relevant support. Activities vary by region and partner.

  1. Identify skills needs. Employers and community partners help define the skills and barriers relevant to their local agricultural workforce.
  2. Connect education and industry. Colleges and training providers work with employers to inform curriculum and training opportunities.
  3. Support career development. Regional partners connect farmworkers and students with career guidance, training, and work-based learning opportunities.

What it offers

What it offers

Training and curriculum development

Collaboration between employers and educators to strengthen agricultural skills education.

Career connections

Guidance and relationships linking farmworkers and students with training and employment pathways.

Work-based learning

Support for internship and apprenticeship opportunities through participating partners.

Regional coordination

Connections among employers, colleges, community organizations, and small farms.
